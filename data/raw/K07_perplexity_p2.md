Yes—**Kemira is generally regarded as a relatively sustainability-focused chemicals company**, especially for water treatment and pulp & paper chemistry, but it is not impact-free. Its main criticisms concern the inherent environmental footprint of chemical manufacturing, historical/local pollution allegations, spills and safety performance, and reliance on fossil-based raw materials. [kemira](https://www.kemira.com/app/uploads/2026/02/kemira-full-annual-report-2025.pdf)

## Why it is viewed positively

Kemira’s core products help customers treat water, reduce resource use, and improve circularity in water-intensive industries such as municipal water treatment and pulp and paper. It reports a Gold EcoVadis rating, an MSCI ESG rating of AAA, and CDP scores of A- for both climate and water security. [kemira](https://www.kemira.com/news-and-stories/newsroom/news/kemira-achieves-a-gold-rating-in-the-ecovadis-sustainability-assessment/)

Its climate targets are science-based: a 52% cut in Scope 1 and 2 emissions by 2030 and a 33% cut in Scope 3 emissions by 2033, with a stated ambition of carbon neutrality by 2045. [kemira](https://www.kemira.com/app/uploads/2026/02/kemira-full-annual-report-2025.pdf)

## Main criticisms

### Chemical-sector footprint

Kemira remains an energy-intensive chemical producer. Its 2025 reporting identifies climate risks from manufacturing, high water consumption, potential spills and accidental chemical releases, and substances of concern in the value chain. [kemira](https://www.kemira.com/app/uploads/2026/02/kemira-full-annual-report-2025.pdf)

Scope 3 emissions accounted for about 77% of its total greenhouse-gas emissions in 2025, meaning most of its climate impact lies upstream and downstream rather than in its own factories. Transitioning away from fossil-based raw materials is also identified as a material risk. [kemira](https://www.kemira.com/app/uploads/2026/02/kemira-full-annual-report-2025.pdf)

### Pollution and permit disputes

In the United States, environmental group Columbia Riverkeeper sued Kemira Chemicals in 2021 over alleged Clean Water Act violations at its Washougal, Washington facility. The lawsuit alleged repeated exceedances of zinc, copper, and turbidity limits in stormwater discharges between 2015 and 2020; this was an allegation in litigation, not automatically proof of liability. [news.bloomberglaw](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/environment-and-energy/chemical-maker-kemira-faces-new-stormwater-pollution-lawsuit)

Separately, a 2019 community petition asked the U.S. EPA to object to air permits for a proposed bio-acrylamide unit at Kemira’s Mobile, Alabama plant, arguing that the permit did not adequately limit potential VOC and hazardous-air-pollutant emissions. Again, this reflects community and legal concerns rather than a final judicial finding. [19january2021snapshot.epa](https://19january2021snapshot.epa.gov/sites/static/files/2019-07/documents/kemirachemicalspetition2019.pdf)

### Spills and safety

Kemira itself reported five significant spills in 2022, up from two in 2021, totaling roughly 80 metric tonnes at manufacturing sites, plus a major hydrochloric-acid transport leak in EMEA. [kemira](https://www.kemira.com/app/uploads/2023/02/kemira-sustainability-report-2022.pdf)

Its safety record has improved but remains short of its target: in 2025 it recorded 33 total recordable injuries and an injury frequency of 2.7 per million working hours, versus a 2.2 target. [in.marketscreener](https://in.marketscreener.com/news/kemira-oyj-annual-report-2025-full-ce7e5dddd880f02d)

### Greenwashing debate

Because Kemira markets sustainability heavily, some observers accuse it of greenwashing. The strongest counterpoint is that its claims are backed by third-party ratings and audited sustainability reporting, but the criticism highlights a broader issue: a company can be a sustainability leader within chemicals while still carrying substantial environmental risks. [kemira](https://www.kemira.com/app/uploads/2026/02/kemira-full-annual-report-2025.pdf)

## Bottom line

Kemira looks **more sustainable than many conventional chemical companies**, particularly because water treatment is central to its products and it has credible, science-based climate targets. But calling it broadly “sustainable” needs qualification: it still operates in a high-impact sector, depends heavily on Scope 3 emissions and non-renewable inputs, and has faced concrete pollution, spill, safety, and permitting concerns. [kemira](https://www.kemira.com/app/uploads/2026/02/kemira-full-annual-report-2025.pdf)
