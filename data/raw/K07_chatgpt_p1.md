# Is Kemira a sustainable company?

My assessment: Kemira is a relatively strong sustainability performer for a chemical company, but it is not an environmentally harmless business. Its biggest strength is that its products help clean water and improve industrial resource efficiency. Its biggest challenge is that manufacturing and supplying industrial chemicals still carries environmental, climate, and chemical-safety risks.

Kemira is a Finnish-listed global chemicals company that supplies water-treatment chemicals and solutions for the pulp, paper, packaging, and other water-intensive industries. Its sustainability therefore needs to be assessed from two perspectives: the benefits its products create for customers and the environmental impacts of making and using those products.

Water treatment, cleaner wastewater, resource efficiency and circular materials.

## Environmental risks

## 2. What are the main criticisms of Kemira?

The strongest criticisms concern the inherent environmental risks of chemical manufacturing, the limits of its climate progress, and whether its sustainability claims reflect the full life cycle of its products.

### 1. Chemical production can create pollution risks

Chemical manufacturing involves substances that must be managed carefully throughout production, transport, use and disposal. Depending on the chemical and process, risks can include hazardous releases, contaminated wastewater, air pollution and industrial waste.

Kemira states that its sites aim for zero releases of hazardous chemicals and zero environmental permit violations. Those are company-stated objectives, not independent proof that every environmental risk has been eliminated.

These are documented compliance concerns, but they should not be confused with evidence that Kemira's entire global operation is systematically polluting the environment. The 2013 case is historical, and its relevance to current performance should be assessed alongside more recent compliance data.

The main criticism is not that Kemira has made no progress. It is that reducing emissions is different from eliminating them. The remaining footprint includes purchased materials, transport and other activities across its value chain.

Scope 3 emissions are especially important because a company can improve the efficiency of its own factories while still depending on carbon-intensive suppliers and logistics.

### 3. Sustainability claims can obscure the full life-cycle impact

Kemira promotes water treatment, circularity and resource efficiency. These benefits are real in principle, but they do not automatically make every chemical it sells sustainable.

A thorough assessment would ask:

- How much energy and raw material are required to manufacture each chemical?
- What happens to the chemical and its by-products after use?
- Does it reduce the customer's overall environmental impact, or merely shift the impact elsewhere?
- Are renewable or recycled inputs environmentally preferable when their entire production and supply chains are considered?

This is an important limitation when evaluating claims such as the proportion of products that improve resource efficiency. A product may improve one environmental indicator while performing less favourably on another.

This is a methodological criticism of how chemical-sector sustainability is measured, rather than evidence that Kemira has been found guilty of greenwashing.

### 4. Its business remains tied to resource-intensive industries

Kemira serves municipal water-treatment operators as well as industrial customers, including the fibre and paper sectors.

Kemira

The water business has an obvious public benefit. The picture is more complicated for chemicals used in industrial production:

- Pulp and paper manufacturing can have significant energy, water, forestry and wastewater impacts.
- Packaging and fibre products can support recycling and replace some fossil-based materials, but they still require raw materials, energy and processing.
- Chemicals can improve industrial efficiency without resolving the broader environmental problems of the industries using them.

The criticism here is about the limits of the business model, not a claim that all Kemira customers or products are unsustainable. Kemira can help make industrial processes cleaner, but it cannot by itself make every end product sustainable.

## 3. Is Kemira greenwashing?

I would distinguish between having a sustainability-led business strategy and being sustainable across the entire value chain.

Kemira has credible evidence supporting its sustainability claims: published climate targets, measurable emissions reductions, external ratings and products that help treat wastewater. I did not find sufficient evidence in the sources reviewed to conclude that its overall sustainability messaging is deliberately misleading.

However, there are legitimate questions worth asking:

- Impact accounting: How much of the claimed water-treatment benefit is attributable to Kemira rather than its customers and other technologies?
- Full environmental footprint: Do its sustainability metrics adequately capture raw-material extraction, chemical hazards, biodiversity and end-of-life impacts?
- Transparency: Are site-level incidents, regulatory breaches and corrective actions disclosed clearly enough for stakeholders to assess performance?
- Transition pace: Is the shift to renewable and recycled materials fast enough to reduce dependence on fossil-based inputs?

Kemira's own reporting identifies resource use, climate change and water as material sustainability topics. Its 2024 report also acknowledged gaps in value-chain traceability for materials with significant biodiversity impacts. That is a concrete area to scrutinise, although it does not by itself establish misconduct or prove that the gap remains unchanged in 2025.

## 4. Overall verdict

## My assessment of Kemira

Strongest area: Water stewardship

Its core products serve an important environmental and public-health need.

Positive: Climate and circularity progress

Emissions have fallen substantially, and the company is increasing renewable and recycled inputs.

Main concern: Chemical-sector environmental risks

Chemical safety, pollution prevention, supply-chain impacts and remaining emissions require continued scrutiny.

Main evidence gap: Independent, site-level verification

Group-level targets and ratings do not tell the whole story about local environmental impacts or individual products.

Bottom line: I would describe Kemira as a company with a credible sustainability strategy and meaningful positive environmental contributions, but with material environmental risks that have not disappeared. It looks stronger than a conventional chemical manufacturer with little evidence of a transition, but I would not call it fully sustainable without further product-level and site-level evidence.

If you are assessing Kemira as a potential employer, supplier or investment, the emphasis should differ: for an employer, workplace safety and company culture matter more; for a supplier, chemical stewardship and product life-cycle impacts matter more; for an investor, emissions targets, regulatory exposure and the commercial growth of genuinely lower-impact products deserve particular attention.
