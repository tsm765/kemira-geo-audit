**Kemira** is a Finnish chemical company that heavily positions itself as a sustainability leader, particularly focused on water treatment, pulp and paper, and renewable packaging solutions.

---

### Is Kemira a sustainable company?

From an operational and strategic standpoint, independent ESG ratings and the company's own reporting point to significant progress:

* **High ESG Ratings:** Kemira has received strong marks from prominent sustainability rating agencies. For instance, it has achieved high ratings from EcoVadis (such as a Platinum rating, placing it among top-tier performers globally) and an AAA rating from MSCI.
* **Science-Based Targets:** Its greenhouse gas reduction targets (covering Scopes 1, 2, and 3) have been officially validated by the Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi).
* **Core Business Alignment:** A large portion of Kemira’s business revolves around water treatment chemicals, municipal wastewater purification, and helping pulp and paper industries reduce plastic use and energy consumption. Over half of its used raw materials are from renewable or recycled sources.

---

### What are the criticisms and challenges?

Like any major chemical manufacturing enterprise, Kemira faces inherent environmental and operational challenges, scrutiny, and criticisms common to the sector:

* **Upstream Value Chain and Scope 3 Emissions:** While Kemira can control its direct factories (Scopes 1 and 2), a significant portion of its environmental footprint lies in its **Scope 3 emissions**—meaning the raw materials, supply chain inputs, and logistics managed by third parties. Sourcing sustainable raw materials at scale without straining other ecosystems remains an ongoing hurdle.
* **Chemical Industry Impacts:** The chemical sector inherently involves the handling, transportation, and processing of hazardous or resource-intensive substances. Critics of chemical manufacturers generally point to risks regarding chemical spills, potential pollution, waste management, and the energy-intensive nature of chemical synthesis.
* **Dependency on Heavy Industry:** Because Kemira's primary markets are pulp, paper, and municipal water infrastructure, its business model is tied to heavy industries that have historically high carbon footprints and resource consumption. Critics sometimes argue that supplying efficiency chemicals to traditional heavy industries is only a half-measure compared to systemic industrial transformation.
* **Resource and Energy Intensity:** Despite utilizing a high percentage of low-carbon energy and recycled materials, large-scale chemical production still demands substantial raw materials, water, and power, leaving it vulnerable to energy price volatility and supply chain disruptions.
