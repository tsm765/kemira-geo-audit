For a European municipal WWTP, start with suppliers that offer **site-specific jar/pilot testing, dewatering polymers, coagulants, dosing control, and local technical service**—not just a catalogue. The strongest shortlist is **Kemira, SNF, Solenis, BASF, and Veolia Water Technologies**. [kemira](https://www.kemira.com/stories/cities-slash-sludge-costs-and-co2/)

## What to ask each supplier

Request a **bench-scale trial first**, then a plant trial on your actual dewatering unit. Ask for:

- Cationic, anionic and blended polymer recommendations for your sludge type (primary, waste activated, digested, lime-conditioned).
- Expected **cake dry solids**, polymer kg/t dry solids, filtrate/centrate TSS and phosphorus return load.
- Compatibility with your equipment: centrifuge, belt filter press, screw press, or filter press.
- Inorganic coagulant options—e.g., ferric salts, PAC/alum—where upstream chemical treatment can improve dewatering and reduce downstream polymer demand. [kemira](https://www.kemira.com/stories/sludge-treatment-great-opportunities-for-improvement/)
- Delivery security, REACH documentation, safety data, storage/make-down requirements, and local emergency/service coverage.
- A performance-based trial with baseline KPIs: wet tonnes hauled, dry solids %, polymer cost, energy use, and centrate quality.

## Practical approach

For most municipal plants, the biggest lever is not merely “more chemical” but selecting the right polymer charge density and molecular weight, then controlling dose against sludge variability. Kemira, Solenis and Veolia all emphasize combining chemistry with monitoring or automated control to improve dewatering and reduce disposal costs. [kemira](https://www.kemira.com/stories/cities-slash-sludge-costs-and-co2/)

A sensible procurement plan is to shortlist **three suppliers**—for example Kemira, SNF and Solenis—run parallel jar tests, then trial the best two at full scale for 4–8 weeks before awarding a supply contract.
