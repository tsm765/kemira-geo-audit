For a municipal wastewater treatment plant in Europe, I’d start by evaluating suppliers of sludge-dewatering and conditioning chemicals, especially polymer flocculants. The right choice depends on whether you want to reduce the volume of sludge leaving the plant, improve mechanical dewatering, or reduce the amount of dry solids produced in the first place.

These are different objectives: dewatering removes water and reduces sludge volume; it does not, by itself, eliminate dry solids.

## Which chemical approach should you prioritise?

A. Increase dewatered cake dryness

Start with cationic polymer optimisation. Compare dry powder and emulsion products using your actual sludge and dewatering equipment. This is generally the first step if you currently pay to transport or dispose of wet sludge.

B. Reduce sludge volume before dewatering

Assess thickening performance and the use of coagulants and flocculants. If sludge is dilute when it reaches the dewatering equipment, upstream optimisation may deliver meaningful savings.

C. Reduce dry-solids production

If your objective is to produce less sludge solids overall, polymer alone will not achieve it. Investigate biological process optimisation and sludge-reduction technologies separately, accounting for energy use, effluent quality, and regulatory constraints.

## How I would run the supplier comparison

Invite at least three suppliers to conduct controlled trials using representative sludge from your plant. Require a report that compares:

- Final cake dry solids (% DS).
- Polymer consumption (kg active polymer per tonne of dry solids).
- Solids capture and centrate/filtrate quality.
- Throughput, downtime and compatibility with existing equipment.
- Total cost per tonne of dry solids processed, including chemical, transport, energy and disposal costs.

For example, if you dewater 1,000 tonnes of sludge cake annually at 20% dry solids, that represents 200 tonnes of dry solids. Raising cake dryness to 25% would reduce cake mass to 800 tonnes annually, assuming the same dry-solids mass is retained. That is a 20% reduction in wet cake mass, before accounting for changes in chemical addition or solids capture.
