Why this matters:

- Chemical manufacturing is energy-intensive. Producing, heating, transporting and processing chemicals requires energy and other resources.
- The supply chain matters enormously. Purchased raw materials are a major source of Kemira's indirect emissions.
- Progress is not the same as achieving the target. Kemira has reduced emissions, but it still needs further reductions to meet its commitments.

Kemira's targets are credible in the sense that they have been validated by SBTi. However, the company has not yet completed its transition to low-carbon production.

This raises two questions:

- How quickly can Kemira replace fossil-based inputs with renewable or recycled alternatives?
- Do its new products actually reduce environmental impacts across their full life cycle, including raw-material sourcing, manufacturing, use and disposal?

Renewable materials are not automatically sustainable. Their impacts depend on how they are produced, how much energy they require and whether they create other environmental pressures.

### 4. Its sustainability claims need to be examined in context

Kemira presents itself as a company that enables clean water and a circular economy. There is a legitimate business rationale for this: its customers need the chemicals it supplies to treat water and manufacture products.

The criticism is not necessarily that this description is false. Rather, the environmental benefits of its customers' activities should not be confused with the environmental performance of Kemira's own operations.

For example, water treated with Kemira's products is a useful impact metric, but it does not by itself tell us:

- How much pollution Kemira generates while manufacturing those products.
- Whether the treated water contains all relevant contaminants at acceptable levels.
- Whether the benefits outweigh the impacts of the chemicals over their life cycle.

A thorough sustainability assessment should examine both the positive impact, often called the handprint, and the negative impact, or footprint.

## 5. What I would investigate further

For a more critical assessment, I would focus on three areas:

1. Site-specific environmental records. Check regulator data for Kemira's individual manufacturing sites, including emissions, spills, permits and enforcement actions. This is especially relevant if you are evaluating a particular factory.
2. Chemical-level risks. Examine its product stewardship disclosures and the substances it manufactures or uses, rather than relying solely on general environmental commitments.
3. Independent verification of its climate and water claims. Look at the scope of external assurance, the calculation methods and whether reported customer benefits reflect measurable net environmental improvements.
