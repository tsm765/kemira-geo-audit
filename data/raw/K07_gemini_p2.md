**Kemira** is a Finnish chemical company that positions itself heavily around sustainability, particularly focusing on water treatment, pulp and paper industries, and renewable packaging solutions.

Whether it is considered a "sustainable company" depends on how you look at its dual role: its products provide critical environmental solutions, but its core operations belong to the chemical industry, which inherently carries environmental footprints.

---

### Why Kemira is Viewed Favorably on Sustainability

1. **Water Treatment and Security:** A major portion of Kemira's business is dedicated to municipal and industrial wastewater treatment and purifying drinking water. Their chemistry helps treat billions of cubic meters of water globally, supporting public health and water recycling.
2. **Transition to Renewables:** Kemira has actively shifted toward bio-based, recyclable, and biodegradable alternatives to replace fossil-based chemicals and plastics (especially in food packaging and hygiene products).
3. **Formal Commitments and Ratings:**
* Their greenhouse gas reduction targets are officially validated by the **Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi)** across scopes 1, 2, and 3.
* They hold high ratings from independent ESG evaluators, such as an EcoVadis Platinum rating (putting them high up in industry benchmarks) and strong MSCI ESG ratings.
* Over half of the raw materials they use are from renewable or recycled sources.



---

### Criticisms and Sustainability Challenges

Despite positive corporate ratings and sustainability goals, Kemira faces the typical criticisms and structural hurdles associated with large-scale chemical manufacturing:

1. **The Inherent Footprint of the Chemical Industry:** Chemical manufacturing is resource-intensive, energy-hungry, and relies heavily on complex supply chains. Even with a push toward renewables, a significant portion of their raw materials and energy consumption still traces back to industrial processes that generate carbon emissions and waste.
2. **Scope 3 Supply Chain Emissions:** Like most chemical producers, a major challenge for Kemira lies in **Scope 3 emissions**—emissions generated upstream by their suppliers and downstream by how customers use or dispose of their products. Managing and reducing these indirect emissions is complex and difficult to completely control.
3. **Chemical Safety and Pollution Risks:** Handling, producing, and transporting industrial chemicals always carries inherent risks of accidental spills, local environmental pollution, or worker safety hazards. Regulatory scrutiny on chemical safety (such as REACH regulations in Europe) requires continuous adaptation to ensure substances do not pose long-term toxicity risks to ecosystems.
4. **"Greenwashing" Skepticism:** Skeptics of corporate sustainability often point out that while companies like Kemira provide green solutions (like water treatment chemicals or paper alternatives), their overall business model remains tied to selling industrial commodities. Critics argue that incremental improvements in a traditionally heavy industry should not mask the fact that chemical synthesis inherently alters natural environments.

### Summary

Kemira is generally recognized by independent rating agencies as an **above-average performer in sustainability** compared to industry peers, largely because its product portfolio actively solves major global issues like water scarcity and plastic waste. However, it remains a heavy industrial chemical company facing ongoing challenges with supply chain emissions, energy intensity, and the inherent risks of chemical production.
