Kemira is **relatively strong on sustainability for a specialty chemicals company**, especially in water treatment, circularity, and climate targets—but it is not impact-free. Its main criticisms stem from the inherent footprint of chemical manufacturing: emissions, energy and water use, pollution/spill risk, hazardous materials, and reliance on fossil-based inputs. [kemira](https://www.kemira.com/app/uploads/2026/02/kemira-full-annual-report-2025.pdf)

## Why it scores well

Kemira’s core business—chemicals for water treatment, pulp and paper, and packaging—can deliver environmental benefits for customers, such as treating, reusing, and recycling water. In 2025, it reported that chemicals it supplied helped treat, reuse, or recycle 21 billion cubic metres of water. [vuosikertomukset](https://www.vuosikertomukset.net/resources/Kemira/fin/vuosikertomukset/Kemira_tilinpaatos_2025.pdf)

It also has credible external signals: an EcoVadis Gold rating with a score of 83/100 in 2026, CDP A- scores for climate and water security, and Science Based Targets initiative-approved emissions targets. [marketscreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/kemira-oyj-half-year-2026-financial-report-ce7f5ed3de8df72d)

| Area | Kemira’s position |
|---|---|
| Climate | Targets a 51.23% cut in Scope 1 and 2 emissions by 2030 from 2018, and a 32.5% Scope 3 cut by 2033 from 2021; aims for carbon neutrality by 2045.  [marketscreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/kemira-oyj-half-year-2026-financial-report-ce7f5ed3de8df72d) |
| Water | Positions water treatment as a major positive impact and has introduced a third-party-verified target for water treated, reused, and recycled.  [marketscreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/kemira-oyj-half-year-2026-financial-report-ce7f5ed3de8df72d) |

| Ratings | EcoVadis Gold, CDP A- for climate and water, and SBTi-approved targets.  [marketscreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/kemira-oyj-half-year-2026-financial-report-ce7f5ed3de8df72d) |

## Main criticisms

- **High-impact sector.** Independent ESG analysis characterizes the materials and chemicals industry as high-risk for climate and pollution, including greenhouse-gas emissions and air, water, and hazardous-waste risks. This limits how “sustainable” any chemicals producer can be considered. [ethifinance](https://www.ethifinance.com/en/ratings/company/1702/8513)

- **Energy-intensive operations.** Kemira identifies its energy-intensive manufacturing as the most significant climate risk to its own operations. Its Scope 1 and 2 emissions are largely tied to energy use. [kemira](https://www.kemira.com/app/uploads/2026/02/kemira-full-annual-report-2025.pdf)

- **Rising emissions intensity.** Ethifinance reported that Kemira’s energy and greenhouse-gas emissions intensities rose by 11% and 20%, respectively, contributing to a year-on-year decline in its environmental score—even though its overall ESG assessment remained positive. [ethifinance](https://www.ethifinance.com/en/ratings/company/1702/8513)

- **Water consumption and effluent.** Kemira itself identifies high water consumption in its operations, along with wastewater discharges, as material negative impacts on ecosystems. [kemira](https://www.kemira.com/app/uploads/2026/02/kemira-full-annual-report-2025.pdf)

- **Spills and chemical-release risk.** The company identifies pollution from potential spills and accidental chemical releases as a material issue. Its older reports also disclosed multiple significant spills; for example, 10 in 2019, including transport and manufacturing incidents. [kemira](https://www.kemira.com/app/uploads/2026/02/kemira-full-annual-report-2025.pdf)

- **Compliance incidents.** In 2023, Kemira reported six environmental, health, and safety fines or penalties—four in the United States, one in Spain, and one in China—totaling about €18,000, plus 15 additional documented non-compliance incidents. The company says the incidents were isolated and corrective actions were completed, but they illustrate ongoing operational compliance risk. [kemira](https://www.kemira.com/app/uploads/2024/02/kemira-ar-sustainability-report-2023.pdf)

- **Fossil and hazardous inputs.** A key transition risk is the inability to move away from fossil-based raw materials. Kemira also acknowledges that substances of concern or very high concern may harm people and the environment across the value chain. [kemira](https://www.kemira.com/app/uploads/2026/02/kemira-full-annual-report-2025.pdf)

- **Waste and circularity setbacks.** In 2025, Kemira generated 73,917 tonnes of manufacturing waste, and its internally tracked recovery rate fell to 42% from 71% in 2024. About 68% of non-recycled waste was incinerated with energy recovery. [csrd.bwdstrategic](https://csrd.bwdstrategic.com/company/kemira?fy=2025)

- **PFAS exposure.** Kemira’s own investor materials note that PFAS chemicals are used in many consumer applications with negative environmental and health impacts. This is a broader industry concern, and companies connected to PFAS chemistry face scrutiny over product safety, liability, and transition plans. [kemira](https://www.kemira.com/app/uploads/2026/04/kemira-oyj_-investor-presentation_april-2026.pdf)

## Balanced view

Kemira looks like a **better-than-average sustainability performer within chemicals**, particularly because water treatment is central to its products and it has measurable, externally recognized climate and water targets. [marketscreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/kemira-oyj-half-year-2026-financial-report-ce7f5ed3de8df72d)

But “sustainable” should be qualified: it remains a chemical manufacturer with material climate, water, waste, pollution, and hazardous-substance risks. The strongest criticisms are not necessarily evidence of unusual misconduct, but rather the structural footprint of its industry and the fact that some metrics—such as emissions intensity and waste recovery—have moved in the wrong direction recently. [ethifinance](https://www.ethifinance.com/en/ratings/company/1702/8513)
