For a municipal wastewater treatment plant in Europe, I’d start by evaluating suppliers that offer sludge dewatering and conditioning chemicals, then look at chemical solutions that reduce the amount of dry solids your plant produces if your objective is to reduce sludge at source.

These are two different goals:

- Reduce sludge volume for disposal: Improve dewatering so the sludge cake contains less water. This is usually the first option to investigate.
- Reduce the quantity of sludge produced: Investigate sludge-reduction processes, such as biological treatment modifications or chemical-assisted sludge disintegration. These can affect energy consumption, effluent quality and operating costs.

The key distinction is that better dewatering reduces wet tonnes transported and disposed of, but does not necessarily reduce the dry-solids mass.

## How to compare supplier offers

Ask each shortlisted supplier to run trials using your actual sludge and report:

- Cake dry solids (%) and the corresponding tonnes of wet cake per day.
- Polymer consumption in kg of active polymer per tonne of dry solids.
- Centrate or filtrate suspended solids.
- Total operating cost per tonne of dry solids processed, including chemicals, disposal and any additional energy.
- Effects on digestion, phosphorus recovery and final sludge disposal or agricultural use, where applicable.

Avoid comparing products solely on price per kilogram. A more expensive polymer may be cheaper overall if it produces a drier cake at a lower dose.
