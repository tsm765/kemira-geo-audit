# What AI assistants say about Kemira

*GEO snapshot · answers collected 20 October 2026 · independent audit, not affiliated with Kemira*

**How this was done.** We asked Perplexity, ChatGPT and Gemini the same ten questions, each twice in separate sessions, logged out, and checked every claim they made about Kemira against 22 Kemira pages and reports captured beforehand. The README has the full method.

## The short answer

The assistants made **123 distinct claims** about Kemira. **78 checked out and three conflicted with Kemira's material.** The rest fall into two groups: 34 that the Kemira material we checked doesn't address, and eight where Kemira's own pages and reports give different figures. The assistants are rarely wrong about Kemira. The gaps are in what the pages don't cover and in figures the pages disagree on.

The more often a claim was repeated, the safer it was. Of the 30 claims all three assistants made in both sessions, **none was wrong**: 26 checked out, three were figures Kemira itself states differently in different places, and one wasn't addressed. None of the errors appeared in a claim more than one assistant made.

## What they get right

All three, in both sessions, get the basic facts right: headquarters, listing, 2025 revenue (about €2.8 billion), headcount (about 4,900) and business units.

Asked three questions about water treatment suppliers that name no company, **every assistant named Kemira in every answer, 18 of 18, and first in 16.** Its main rival, SNF, was also named in all 18, but first in only two, so what sets Kemira apart is being named first. Gemini named SNF first in two of the three questions in its second session, after putting Kemira first in its first.

## What they get wrong

All three errors came from Gemini. It said, in both sessions, that Kemira holds an EcoVadis Platinum sustainability rating; Kemira reports Gold. It garbled Kemira's list of sustainability priorities. And it said Kemira aims for carbon neutrality "overall". Kemira's 2045 target covers its own operations and the energy it buys; emissions from its supply chain sit under a separate reduction target.

## Where the assistants disagree, and why

Assistants gave Kemira's main climate target as either a 52% emissions cut by 2030 or 51.23%. Both are Kemira's figures: its climate page says 52%, its financial reports say 51.23%. **Whichever figure an assistant gave, it matched one Kemira source and contradicted another**, so the divergence starts in Kemira's own material. A second climate target and two target dates split the same way. One ChatGPT answer said "approximately 51–52%", spanning both.

## What is missing

For 34 claims, the Kemira material we checked has nothing to confirm or contradict them. 16 of the 34 came from one question, "Is Kemira a sustainable company? What are the criticisms?", where assistants raised reputational and regulatory material that Kemira's pages don't discuss.

## Where the assistants look, and what they find

**106 of the 208 links the assistants gave point to kemira.com**, so Kemira's own site is their main source. But they reach different pages from the ones built to answer the question: they cited 25 Kemira pages outside our set, while 12 of the 22 we checked were never cited. **The sludge page was never cited, even in answers about reducing sludge.**

**Seven of 21 pages open with a date rather than an answer, and those seven are the whole investor section.** No page carries the hidden label that tells software it describes a company, and 19 of 21 have no title heading in their code. Every page lacks the same things. Of the unresolved claims we could trace to a page, most belong to two: the sustainability page (10 claims, made 29 times across the answers) and the sludge page (5 claims, made 12 times).
